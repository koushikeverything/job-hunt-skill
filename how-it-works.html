Job Hunt, Backstage

You hand over a CV; twenty-odd minutes later you get a spreadsheet of real, open, scored job opportunities. This page walks through each step twice: what you see on stage, and what the machinery does behind the curtain — in plain terms.

On stage — what you do & see Backstage — what the skill actually does
1

You share your CV and answer a short form

On stage

Attach your resume (or a LinkedIn URL) and answer up to four quick questions: where you want to work, what to exclude (internships, night shifts, commission-only…), and which job boards you're willing to log into.

That login question comes now, on purpose — logged-in boards like LinkedIn are some of the richest sources, and asking upfront means none of them are dead ends later.

Backstage

The CV is parsed into structured facts: years of experience per skill, education, languages, and — crucially — your exact city and state plus work-authorisation status. Many "remote" jobs are only open to certain states or countries, and this is what makes those checks possible later.

It also opens a run manifest — a flight recorder that every following step writes into, so the final report can honestly say what was searched, what wasn't, and why.

2

Your profile becomes ~40 job titles — not just one

On stage

A progress checklist appears and starts ticking. Nothing is needed from you here.

The best find of a run usually comes from this step: a great role hiding under a title you'd never have typed into a search box.

Backstage

The skill detects your profession archetype (product-tech, healthcare, NGO…) and loads a matching playbook of boards and employers. Then it expands your title three ways: direct matches, adjacent titles ("what is this job called at a company 10× smaller? which other department owns this work?"), and stretch roles — plus searches by requirement text, not just titles, to catch mislabeled roles.

It also samples ~25 live postings and measures the real years-of-experience bar for your market — so "within reach" vs "a stretch" is based on data, not a guess.

3

Every source is tested before it's trusted

On stage

You might get one more question: "board X needs a login — open it in Chrome?" or "connect Y, or proceed without?" Answer it once and the run continues.

Backstage

Before any real searching, the skill fires one test query at every job-site connection and board and records the result: works, blocked in your country, needs a login, or unreachable. Dead sources are dropped so no searcher wastes time discovering the same failure three times.

It also builds a target list of 150–300 companies likely to be hiring you — prolific hirers in your field, recently funded companies in your domains, and any employer you flagged as interesting.

4

A fleet of searchers fans out — and loops until dry

On stage

This is the long stretch — 15–25 minutes of the checklist ticking as parallel searches run.

Backstage

Six searcher agents run at once, each owning a different channel: job-site connections, remote boards, community and investor job boards, a logged-in browser sweep of LinkedIn, a creative "roamer" chasing the adjacent titles — and the star channel: polling company career pages directly. One visit to a company's own careers page reveals everything it has open; in real runs this channel produced 4× more top-tier finds per lookup than any job aggregator.

When a pass finds plenty, another pass runs with those results excluded — repeating until two passes in a row come up nearly empty. One pass is never enough.

5

Every role is checked at the source, then scored

On stage

Invisible to you — but it's why every link in your spreadsheet works and every "apply now" is genuinely open. No role is ever invented; anything unknown says "not listed".

Backstage

A role only counts if it was seen on the employer's own website — not just a copy on some aggregator, which real runs showed are wrong shockingly often. Each row carries an authenticity grade (A–X) saying exactly how it was confirmed. Duplicates are merged (the same job on three boards is one row).

Then two separate scores: Fit (does the work match you?) and Attainability (will you actually get it — seniority bar, location rules, hard requirements?). A ghost-job check flags postings that are technically live but probably not real hiring: reposted five times, thousands of applicants, vague evergreen wording.

6

A skeptic tries to knock out the winners

On stage

Nothing visible — this is pure quality control. What survives this step is what you see as a "top pick".

Backstage

Fresh agents are briefed with one job: prove the top picks wrong. They re-open every would-be "apply now" row and try to show it's expired, closed to your state or country, missing a required licence or language, or attributed to the wrong company. In early runs this audit corrected 63% of aggregator-sourced rows — which is why it's never skipped.

If fewer than five roles survive as "apply now", the skill says so, diagnoses why, and runs one targeted extra pass — it never quietly delivers a thin list.

7

You get the workbook — and a four-paragraph brief

On stage

An Excel workbook lands: All Roles (scored, graded, deduped), Top Picks & Deadlines, Leads to check manually, CV keyword gaps, and a Coverage Report. Every row has a next action: apply, confirm it's still open, or ask the recruiter about location.

The brief on top: headline counts, 5–8 standouts with one-line reasons, deadlines in the next 14 days, and what needs you.

Backstage

The Coverage Report is the honesty page: every source it tried, what it yielded, and what it couldn't search (login walls, blocked sites) — which is exactly the list you can fix before the next run. The keyword-gap list comes from comparing your CV against the actual text of every posting fetched: the terms employers keep asking for that your CV doesn't say.

8

Run it again — see only what's new

On stage

Say "new roles since last time" — or schedule it daily or weekly. You get only fresh rows, and your existing shortlist is kept honest: expired roles get marked, not silently left as dead links.

Backstage

The workbook doubles as the skill's memory. A rerun loads it, excludes everything already delivered, re-checks your active "apply now" rows, and polls the company list first — the cheapest, freshest channel, since prolific hirers change weekly.

The system learns: a source that yields nothing three runs in a row gets demoted; one that produced an apply-now role gets checked more often. Each run is cheaper than the last.

Four rules it will not break

  • Never invents. No role, salary, deadline, or link is ever made up — unknown fields say "not listed".
  • Verified at the source. Every "apply now" row was confirmed on the employer's own website, twice.
  • No padding. A longer list comes from searching more channels — never from including expired, ineligible, or ghost postings.
  • Honest about gaps. What couldn't be searched, and why, is reported — not hidden.
